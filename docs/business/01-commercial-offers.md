# Commercial Offers

## Offer Principles

- B2B institutional revenue is the primary business engine.
- B2C micro-subscription keeps access possible for users outside covered institutions.
- Sponsored access funds impact and adoption.
- Pricing must support FCFA, Mobile Money, and annual institutional invoicing.
- Reading access, download access, and offline access are separate commercial rights.

## B2B Institutional Offers

B2B offers target universities, schools, training institutions, enterprises, foundations, and public bodies that want to fund access for a defined population.

Initial offer families:

- Institution license: annual or semester-based access for a defined number of active users.
- Quota license: prepaid quota of seats, activations, or reading entitlements.
- Domain license: access limited to selected academic domains or collections.
- Archive digitization and hosting package: ingestion and controlled distribution of an institution's own documentary fund.
- Sponsored institution access: a third-party sponsor pays for an institution or cohort.

B2B contracts should define covered users, access duration, collection scope, reporting rights, support level, renewal terms, and data/privacy boundaries.

## B2C Micro-Subscriptions

B2C offers target individuals not covered by an institution. This layer is for accessibility and market proof, not the main revenue engine.

Possible formats:

- Daily pass.
- Weekly pass.
- Monthly pass.
- Exam-period pass.
- Domain-specific pass.
- Freemium discovery with restricted reading unlocked by paid entitlement.

B2C payments should prioritize Mobile Money and simple renewal flows. Every B2C entitlement must be technically compatible with the same access-control system used by institutional users.

## Sponsored Access

Sponsored access lets a partner finance access for a group: students of a university, a filiere, a city, a scholarship cohort, or a national campaign.

Sponsor value:

- Measurable education impact.
- Brand association with access to knowledge.
- Usage reports tied to funded access.
- Potential public relations and institutional goodwill.

Sponsor reporting must be aggregated unless an explicit legal basis allows more detail.

## Content Revenue Sharing

Revenue sharing applies only when content rights require it. Possible models:

- Fixed license fee to a publisher, author, or institution.
- Revenue share based on subscriptions attributed to a collection.
- Usage-based allocation using auditable reading metrics.
- Sponsored content fund paid by a partner.

Contracts must define calculation basis, payment period, reporting detail, withdrawal terms, and tax/payment handling.

## Pricing Inputs To Validate

Before final pricing, validate:

- Student willingness to pay per day, week, month, and exam period.
- Institution budget cycles and procurement constraints.
- Cost per active reader, including storage, bandwidth, processing, support, and payment fees.
- Average content acquisition cost by category.
- Mobile Money transaction fees and failure rates.
- Sponsor appetite for cohort-based impact packages.
- Whether download/offline rights should be premium features.

## Acceptance Criteria

- The offer model supports B2B, B2C, and sponsored access without separate product silos.
- Institutional contracts can map to quotas and entitlements.
- Individual subscriptions can coexist with organization access.
- Sponsored access can be measured without exposing personal reading data unnecessarily.
- Content revenue sharing is contract-driven and auditable.
